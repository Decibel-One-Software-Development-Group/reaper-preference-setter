Now **SiRPS**, on the Decibel One pipeline: the suite icon, hand-written release
notes, and a landing page of its own.

## Records over MADI, not just a SoundGrid card

A session that records to MADI was refused outright — "The console doesn't
appear to have a Reaper/SoundGrid card configured" — even when the Copy Audio
preset was perfectly good. On MADI the record ports carry the console's device
prefix, `1:MADI 1`, and the rule for a record port required the name to start
with a letter. Nothing matched, no destination resolved to a Reaper input, and
the whole session failed. MADI now reads the same as a SoundGrid card.

## You choose the ports you record to

Copy Audio names its own destination, so the app finds that port by itself. It
could never find a **second** stream — a program mix, press feeds, a matrix
patched straight to an output — because nothing in Copy Audio refers to them.
Those channels were simply missing from the CSV with nothing to say so.

The DiGiCo tab now lists the record outputs it can see, with what is on each
one, and you tick the ports you record to. The track count updates as you tick.
Everything patched to any ticked port lands in one CSV.

Ports are laid out in console order, each taking its full width, which is how
the interface hands them to Reaper: MADI 1 holds inputs 1-64 even when only
1-62 are patched, and MADI 2 starts at 65. Unpatched channels come through as
blank rows, and that is what keeps the numbering honest.

The racks Copy Audio **reads from** are kept off the list on purpose. Reverb and
aux returns get patched back out to those same Dante ports, so offering them
would invite effects returns into the track list.

Record cards are unaffected: two that number straight through — `Trks 1-64`
then `Tracks 65-128` — still land where their own numbering says.

## More tracks arrive with their real name

- Anything the Copy Audio preset copies is now an input by definition. Channels
  patched from ports an engineer had renamed outright — `P.A Trk 1 L`, `KCmp 1`,
  `Orch Sum L/R` — resolved no route at all and fell back to a raw port name.
  On one 130-track session that moved 105 channels to 124.
- Stereo channels pair by port order instead of by bumping a trailing number in
  the port name, so a channel on a named stagebox socket (`S4-4 K1 L`) gets its
  `.R` row rather than leaving a raw port name on the next track.
- Where several channels legitimately claim one input — a tech-listen channel
  fed from the same playback as the primary — the lowest-numbered channel wins
  it, that being the primary by desk convention.
- Sessions from older Quantum software (`vM`) parse, and a rig whose record card
  isn't called "Waves" is no longer assumed away.

## Looks like the suite, ships like the suite

The element-tile icon and the SiRPS name. Signed, notarized and stapled as
before, on both Apple Silicon and Intel from one universal download.

There is a page of its own now, and the app has an **About** box and
**Check for Updates…** — in the application menu on macOS, under Help on
Windows. It reads the same Sparkle appcast the rest of the suite publishes,
tells you when a newer build exists, and opens the download. Installing stays
a drag to Applications: replacing a running app from inside itself is where
updaters go wrong, and this is a tool you open a few times a show.

Release notes are written by hand now, and a tag without them fails the build
rather than shipping a release nobody can read.
